# Collaborative Discussion 1: Initial Post

## Discussion Topic
The Data Collection Process: A Critical Evaluation of the Internet of Things (IoT)

## Initial Post

The Internet of Things (IoT) presents substantial opportunities for organizations to enhance the quality of their decision-making through the systematic collection of data and its analysis in real time. However, just because there is a great number of data collected does not mean that there is a provision of good insights. The value of IoT should be examined not simply by the volume of information generated, but by its reliability, relevance and contribution to meaningful decisions. In organizations, IoT provides resource optimisation, proactive monitoring and early identification of operational inefficiencies. For example, the implementation of connected meters in smart water networks can help organizations in identifying unexpected consumption behaviours, thereby providing more informed decisions regarding water resource utilisation. Nevertheless, achieving these advantages depends on the reliability of the collected information in accurately representing the conditions under observation.

The process of data cleaning is essential for identifying incomplete records, irregularities and extreme values that may compromise the validity and of data analysis (Huxley, 2020). In large-scale IoT systems, these challenges are important, as the continuous collection of data can enhance vast amounts of information without necessarily modifying its quality. The principle of data minimisation requires personal data collection to be limited to what is necessary for specified purposes (European Parliament and Council, 2016). This is relevant to IoT systems that capture information continuously about individual behaviour. Furthermore, the adoption of IoT technologies requires a balanced assessment of their associated risks and potential benefits as greater technological efficiency does not necessarily translate into increased organizational value (Brous, Janssen and Herder, 2020).
