# Collaborative Discussion 1: Peer Response 1

## Discussion Topic
The Data Collection Process

## Peer Response

Thank you for sharing your analysis that provides a compelling examination of the unintended security and privacy implications associated with large-scale IoT data collection, as illustrated by the Strava incident. This example highlights how data gathered for legitimate objective can introduce serious security concerns when aggregated and shared with the public. I particularly agree with your argument that individuals may not have control over how their information is used. A key consideration is the extent to which organizations evaluate the potential implications of data collection before introducing IoT technologies into their operations. Although devices that are connected can provide valuable insights, the Strava incident demonstrates that removing direct personal identifiers does not eradicate privacy risks. Although location data may be combined, it can still disclose sensitive behavioural trends when the recorded observations are linked to identifiable geographical locations.

One preventative measure would be to conduct privacy impact assessments before sharing practices or introducing new data collection. The General Data Protection Regulation illustrates data protection by default and design, which requires organizations to include appropriate safeguards into their processing activities (European Parliament and Council, 2016). In practice, this could involve restricting the sensitive location information being public, limiting access to detailed movement records and applying appropriate aggregation thresholds. Your reference to Huxley (2020) also raised an important issue that concern transparency. Although data cleaning can improve analytical reliability, it cannot independently resolve ethical concerns surrounding the collection and subsequent use of personal information. Overall, organizations must therefore distinguish between determining whether their use of data is ethically justified and ensuring that their data is technically accurate.
